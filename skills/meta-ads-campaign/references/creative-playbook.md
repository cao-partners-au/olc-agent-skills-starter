# Meta creative playbook (2025+ ranking system)

Adapted from coreyhaines31/marketingskills (MIT), `ads/references/meta-andromeda-playbook.md`.

Meta's "Andromeda" ranking system (2025) decides who sees an ad largely from the ad itself. The
main lever is now **how many genuinely different ads you feed it**, not audience settings.

## 1. Broad audience, specific creative
- Target the whole country (or your real service area) and let the ad find the person. Narrow
  interest stacks and small radius circles usually underperform now.
- If your ads fall under a Special Ad Category (employment, housing, credit, social issues),
  targeting is restricted anyway, so creative is your **only** real targeting lever.
- **Longer primary text helps**: name the customer, their industry or situation, and the problem.
  It gives Meta more to read about who the ad is for.

## 2. Identity keywords
- Duplicate a winning ad with the reader's identity in the headline or first line:
  "…for **dentists**", "…for **tradies**", "…for **first-home buyers**", "…for **gym owners**".
- The keyword tells the viewer "this is for me" and tells Meta who to show it to.
- Pick identities from your best existing customers, not from guesses.

## 3. Volume over polish: statics first
- Meta can show more statics per session and they cost far less to make. A steady flow of plain
  statics usually beats a few expensive videos.
- **Rule:** budget about one hour a week to make 3 to 5 new static concepts for your current best
  offer. Prove a concept as a static before paying for a video version (exception: testimonials and
  demos, which are naturally video).

## 4. Concepts, not copies
- Meta groups ads it judges too similar (practitioners put it around 60% similarity) and limits
  their delivery. Ten variants of one ad are **one concept** and can steal reach from the original.
- Plan every new ad on three axes and log it:
  - **concept:** the single truth it argues ("done in a day", "no lock-in contract")
  - **angle:** how the benefit is framed (time, money, status, safety, ease)
  - **awareness:** new to the problem vs already comparing options
- AI rewrites of a winner for a new audience (same ad, new identity words) are for **localising** a
  proven concept, not for testing volume.

## 5. Zombie ad set
- In any ad set Meta funds a few ads and starves most. Ads you still believe in that got very little
  spend can be moved into a small separate ad set to get a real read. Some come back as winners.
- Don't run more ads than the budget can feed (see the ad-count ceiling in `meta-weekly-review`).

## 6. Native, not "ad-looking"
- Polished agency-style ads read as ads and get scrolled. Match what already performs organically
  in your niche: owner or staff talking to camera, phone-shot, real screenshots, real numbers.
- If an organic post or video performs, run that exact post as an ad.
- Before spending, open the ad preview **on a phone, inside Instagram/Facebook's in-app browser**
  and tap through: page loads, the promise matches the ad, the form submits, tracking fires. Desktop
  doesn't count.

## 7. Judge on outcomes, not form fills
- Optimise toward the event that predicts a paying customer once you have enough of it (roughly 50 a
  week per ad set). Until then, optimise for leads but **judge** ads on what the leads did in your
  CRM (booked, showed, bought).
- Give each new ad about five days before judging it; Friday launches meet a different audience
  than Monday ones.

## 8. Keep a lessons file
- After each weekly review, add one line per finished test: concept, angle, identity, result.
  Brief the next batch of statics from it.
